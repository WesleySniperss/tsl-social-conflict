# TSL: Social Conflict

Social conflict for **Foundry VTT v13+** (v14 verified), built for **dnd5e** and **a5e** (Level Up). You want something from an NPC who doesn't want to give it, and the person matters more than the dice. Every NPC has a hidden **psychological nature**: a **key** that opens them, a **wall** they close against, and a **price** they name when they give in. Read them first, and a modest character beats a silver tongue who never looked. Many wear a **mask**, and some bend or harden **before witnesses**. Inspired by the emotional mechanics of **Thirsty Sword Lesbians**, with a court-intrigue edge:

> *Every word is a move. Every favour is a debt. Everyone wears a mask — and someone is always watching.*

The module speaks **English and Ukrainian** (a per-user setting).

## Seven techniques

No turns, no tracks, no declared goal. **Target** the NPC you're talking to on the map (hover their token, press **T**), say your line, pick the **technique** it really is, and roll once against a **visible DC = 10 + their proficiency + the better of WIS/INT**. The roll goes through the system's own skill dialog.

| | Pressure | Heart | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ask** — get it now | **Demand** — do it, or else | **Appeal** — ask from the heart | **Bargain** — make it worth their while |
| **All in** — a gamble on leverage | **Blackmail** — their Fear, or your hold on them | **Seduce** — their Desire, or what's between you | **Deceive** — a lie that works, until it doesn't |
| **Read them** — learn something | *no approach* — watch, listen, ask the one right question | | |

- **Asks** are sized by what they cost *them*:

  | Size | What it is | DC |
  |---|---|---|
  | Small | a favour, a door | −4 |
  | Costly | something it costs them to give | — |
  | Against them | against their interests or loyalties | +4 |

  An ask against them needs leverage: their Desire or Fear, a String on them, or a deep bond — or go all in.
- **All in** works at any size and names no price, but it leaves a lasting mark:
  - **Blackmail** plays on their Fear: they do it, and they're your enemy now.
  - **Seduce** plays on their Desire or your bond: they do it for you.
  - **Deceive** works until the GM brings the lie out — then they turn on you.
- **Read them** answers one question for the whole party. A strong hit answers a second, or keeps a String on them instead (you saw where it hurts). A missed Read never costs you an emotion.

What winning costs depends on the approach:

| Approach | When it works | When it misses |
|---|---|---|
| **Pressure** | They give in without a price, but remember it: the bond sours, and they take a String on you. | Nothing extra. |
| **Heart** | The bond warms. | You exposed yourself: they take a String on you. |
| **Reason** | A clean deal; "yes, but" names their price. | Nothing extra. |

What they say:

| Result | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong hit** | 5 over, or a 20 | It works. An ask or gambit for something big also hands you a String on them. |
| **Hit** | a hit | It works. An Appeal or a Bargain gets "yes, but": their nature names the price; you pay, or you walk away. |
| **Miss** | a miss | They push back their own way, and it hurts: you take their nature's **emotion** (anything but a Read). A proud nature, refused something big, closes the door for the scene. |
| **Bad miss** | 5 under, or a 1 | As a miss, and they take a String on you as well. |

**In private, or before witnesses.** Before an ask or a gambit, say who is watching. Before witnesses a yes binds them (a String on them: everyone saw it), and a no shames you (you lose face: Insecure). And the room tips the roll: some natures bend before an audience (+2), most harden (−2). Like the key and the wall, that shows only in the result, or in a Read.

After a miss you may burn a String on them for +5. Your roll adds:
- the skill that fits your words;
- +1 per Leanings dot in that approach;
- +● for a bond that runs that way;
- +2 for their known Desire or Fear;
- +2 when a friend helps.

## Emotions and scars

Five emotions, as in Thirsty Sword Lesbians. Anyone can carry them, a player character or an NPC. They show as status icons on the token; the GM can also toggle them from the Token HUD.

| Emotion | On whoever feels it | For whoever plays on it | Let it go |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Angry** | −2 on Heart | +2 to Pressure against them | lash out: hurt someone, or break something |
| **Frightened** | −2 on Pressure | +2 to Pressure against them | back down, or flee what scares you |
| **Guilty** | −2 on everything against the one you wronged | +2 to Heart from the one they wronged | make amends |
| **Insecure** | −2 on Reason | +2 to Heart against them | prove yourself with a reckless risk |
| **Hopeless** | −2 on asks and gambits | +2 to Reason against them | give something up, or accept comfort |

A friend's comfort lets any of them go, too. Where emotions come from:
- **a miss**: each nature hits back with its own emotion (the Tyrant frightens you, the Duelist makes you angry, the Martyr makes you guilty…);
- **a String** spent on someone (Bonds → Hurt): players do it to NPCs, and the GM's NPCs do it to players;
- **gambits**: Blackmail leaves them Frightened; a broken heart leaves you Hopeless; a lie brought out leaves them Angry;
- **a no before witnesses**: you lose face, and you're Insecure.

Take an emotion you already carry, and it leaves a **scar** instead. A scar is permanent, one per emotion, and a twist rather than just a wound:

| Scar | From | For good |
|---|---|---|
| Short Fuse | Angry | +1 Pressure, −1 Heart |
| Flinch | Frightened | +1 on Reads, −1 Pressure |
| Debt | Guilty | +1 Heart, −1 Reason |
| Two Faces | Insecure | +1 on Gambits, −1 on Reads |
| Hollow | Hopeless | +1 Reason, −1 Heart |

## Nine natures

The natures follow Karen Horney's three ways of coping with people:
- moving **against** them — Power: Tyrant, Schemer, Duelist;
- moving **toward** them — Emotion: Martyr, Idol, Caretaker;
- moving **away** from them — Order: Zealot, Hermit, Broker.

Each nature is grounded in a real character type. Each has:
- a **key** approach: on both of its techniques (the ask and the gambit) you roll a second d20 and keep the better;
- a **wall** approach: a second d20, keep the worse;
- its own **price** for a "yes, but";
- its own pushback on a no, and the emotion it leaves on you when you miss;
- whether it keeps listening after refusing something big;
- how it takes an audience: before witnesses it bends (+2), hardens (−2), or doesn't care.

The second die is rolled after the first, so nothing before the roll gives the nature away; the result does. Inside a triad all three key/wall pairs differ, so knowing someone's way is never enough.

**Reading people.** A hit answers one question for the whole party:
- what they want;
- what they fear;
- what would open them;
- what would close them;
- what they'd want in return;
- whether they end it after a no;
- whether the face they show is their true one;
- whether they bend or harden before witnesses.

The party keeps every answer. Once it knows the key and the wall, the nature is named and its whole card opens.

**Masks** (Nature and Demeanor). The GM can give an NPC a face to show: their own nature (an open face), another one (a mask: a Schemer playing the Caretaker), or nothing (unreadable). Players see "seems: Caretaker" in Talk and on the Scene, and can open how they seem; the key, the wall and the price are always the true nature's. A Read ("Is that their true face?") names an open face on the spot, or shows a mask for what it is; learning the key and the wall names them, and the mask falls.

## Strings and bonds

- **Strings** are a hold on one person, at most 3 each. A String can be:
  - spent for **+5** after a miss against them;
  - spent to **Hurt** them: make them feel an emotion;
  - used as leverage for an ask against their interests, or for Blackmail;
  - **Pulled** in a fight. Strike gives +5 to your next attack, check or save against them. Guard gives +5 AC against their next attack. Either one is a real effect on your sheet that ends itself.

  A String is a debt, a weakness seen, or a line crossed in front of others. You gain one on them when they give more than they meant to (a strong hit on something big), when they give before witnesses, when a strong Read sees where it hurts, and when they open up to you in play (the GM hands it over). They gain one on you when you win by Pressure or Blackmail, when your heart is refused, when your threat or lie is caught, when you pay a price that is a debt, and on a bad miss. At most one changes hands each way per roll. A String on someone you have no bond with makes you Strangers, so it shows in Bonds, ready to pull.
- **Bonds** are one shared relationship per pair, mirrored on both actors: a type (friend, lover, rival, enemy, sworn, confidant…) and a strength ●–●●●.
  - A bond gives +1 per ● on techniques of the approach it runs: Heart for love and family, Pressure for rivals and enemies, Reason for allies, oaths and trust.
  - Heart wins warm bonds (a stranger becomes a Friend).
  - Pressure wins sour them (a stranger becomes a Rival).
  - Blackmail and exposed lies make Enemies.
  - Slammed doors and broken hearts cool bonds.

## Windows

- **Chronicle**: right-click a token, then the address-book button. It has four tabs:
  - *Profile*: an NPC's nature, the mask they show, dossier and Agenda for the GM, and what the party has learned for players; a player character's Leanings, emotions and scars;
  - *Bonds*;
  - **Talk**: who you're talking to (your target on the map) and the face they show, the seven techniques, in private or before witnesses, your emotions and theirs, the roll. On an NPC's own sheet this tab is the GM's cheat sheet: key, wall, price, pushback, the lies they believe, and buttons to ask them as any character on the scene;
  - *Codex*: the in-game manual.
- **Social Scene**: the **Social** button on the VTools toolbar. It is a live relationship map for everyone: portraits, bonds as lines, emotions, named natures and the faces NPCs show, and every roll animated as it happens. *This scene* is for the table; *All bonds* is for the GM.

## Settings

- **Language / Мова** (each user's own): Auto follows Foundry's language; or English, or Ukrainian. It switches at once. Chat cards are written in the language of whoever creates them (the GM's client resolves rolls).
- A global social-difficulty dial, and whether rolls use the system's own skill dialog.

Updating to 3.3 adds two script files: **restart the world** (not just F5) after updating.

Upgrading from 2.x: the first world load removes the old v2 states, wounds, scars and auras from sheets; v3's emotions and scars replace them. Bonds, Strings, dossiers and natures are kept.

Requires the **vtools** module.
