# TSL: Social Conflict

Social conflict for **Foundry VTT v13+** (v14 verified), built for **dnd5e** and **a5e** (Level Up). You want something from an NPC who doesn't want to give it, and the person matters more than the dice. Every NPC has a hidden **psychological nature**: a **key** that opens them, a **wall** they close against, and a **price** they name when they give in. Read them first, and a modest character beats a silver tongue who never looked. Many wear a **mask**, and some bend or harden **before witnesses**. Inspired by the emotional mechanics of **Thirsty Sword Lesbians**, with a court-intrigue edge:

> *Every word is a move. Every favour is a debt. And someone is always watching.*

The module speaks **English and Ukrainian** (a per-user setting).

## A conversation is played

You want something from someone who doesn't want to give it. No turns, no tracks, no score — and the conversation itself isn't run by the module: **you play it**. The players talk in character with someone on the map (hover their token, press **T**); the GM answers as them.

1. **Talk.** The GM plays their hidden nature: one approach opens them, another closes them — the NPC's own Talk tab says how they take each one. Watch how they take what you say: that's how you read them.
2. **What the talk does, stays — no roll.** When something comes out (what they want, what they fear, their price), the GM marks it and the party knows it from then on. When your words land — a threat that frightens, a reproach that shames, a truth with no way out — the GM marks the emotion it leaves on them: +2 to the approach that plays on it, until they act it out.
3. **The roll — when the GM calls for it.** Usually once, at the moment that decides it; at most two or three times a conversation. Talk asks what you're doing — *I persuade*, *I intimidate*, *I lie* or *I read them*, the four social checks of D&D — and what happened: how your words came at them, plainly or all in, what you asked for, who saw it, what you played on, who backed you — and counts all of it.

Each ask is its own yes or no. What carries over is what you learn about them, the emotions you leave, the Strings and bonds.

## Ten maneuvers

When the GM calls for the roll, say what your words really were, and roll once against a **visible DC = 10 + their proficiency + the better of WIS/INT**. **Every maneuver is a pair of D&D skills**: the better of the two rolls, in the system's own check window (a5e: expertise dice and effects count; change the ability or add dice there; on dnd5e the module rolls the d20 itself, with the same math); the other **backs it (+ your proficiency)** if you're trained in it. Apart from your Leanings (below), the module keeps no stats of its own — your sheet is your character, and your Profile lists what you're best at.

| Button | Maneuvers |
|---|---|
| **I persuade** | **Appeal** (Heart) — Persuasion + Performance · **Argument** (Reason) — Persuasion + History · all in: **Seduce** (Heart) — Persuasion + Performance. Reason has no all-in: a promise is part of the talk |
| **I intimidate** | **Demand** (Pressure) — Intimidation + Athletics · all in: **Blackmail** (Pressure) — Intimidation + Investigation |
| **I lie** | **Deceive**, told any way — Deception + Performance: a threat you can't back up, feelings you don't have, proof that isn't |
| **I read them** | four ways, when the talk won't tell: **Watch** (Insight + Investigation, *no approach*, safe), **Provoke** (Intimidation + Insight, Pressure), **Confide** (Persuasion + Insight, Heart), **Question** (Investigation + History, Reason) |

- **Asks** are sized by what they cost *them*:

  | Size | What it is | DC |
  |---|---|---|
  | Small | a favour, a door | −4 |
  | Costly | something it costs them to give | — |
  | Against them | against their interests or loyalties | +4 |

  Nothing is locked. Against their interests is 4 harder, and you say what makes them listen — their Desire or Fear, a String on them, a deep bond, something from the story; the GM decides whether it's worth a roll. With nothing of it on record, the form says so.
- **All in** works at any size and is never locked: what you hold over someone may have come from anyone, so the table decides whether it bites. What the party has on record adds +2. It always leaves a lasting mark:
  - **Blackmail** plays on what you hold over them — their Fear (+2 once known), a secret, a debt: they do it, Frightened of you, and they'll pay you back.
  - **Seduce** plays on their Desire (+2 once known), on your bond, or on charm alone: they do it for you, no price — and you let them close: they take a String on you.
- **A lie isn't an approach — it's told in one.** Press *I lie* and say how you told it, and how big it is (a small lie is 4 easier, a bold one 4 harder): their key and wall, your bond and their emotions all count for the way you told it. A sob story to someone who opens to Heart rolls with advantage; forged proof to someone who closes against Reason, with disadvantage. **Deceive** wins no String, however well it lands: what they believe is no hold on them. It works until the lie comes out — the GM brings it out, or a bad miss of yours against them does — and then they turn on you.
- **Read them** answers one question for the whole party; a strong hit, two. What can be seen in the moment is told straight: whether it's true, what they're after right now, how they'd take Pressure, Heart or Reason, how they act before witnesses. What they hide is never told: their Desire, Fear or Price. A hit shows it — the card carries a line of what you see (below) — and once the players name it, the GM marks it known with one click. Anything can also come out of the talk with no roll at all. A Read never moves Strings, and a missed one never costs you an emotion. Naming someone's nature opens its card, but not what they themselves want and fear.

**Every roll is a stake.** Win an ask or an all-in by 5 or more (or roll a 20), and you take a **String** on them; miss by 5 or more (or roll a 1), and they take one on you. The same rule both ways, one String at a time each way. A roll never changes a bond. What winning also costs depends on the approach:

| Approach | When it works | When it misses |
|---|---|---|
| **Pressure** | They give in without a price, but remember it: they take a String on you, even when you win. Pressing someone close to you leaves you Guilty. | Their emotion. |
| **Heart** | Their price. | You showed your heart: their emotion, and they take a String on you — on any miss, not only a bad one. |
| **Reason** | They see it your way, for their price. | Their emotion. |

What they say:

| Result | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong hit** | 5 over, or a 20 | It works, and it's worth a String on them. An Appeal's or an Argument's price still stands: pay it and keep the String, or put the String on it and have it on your terms. |
| **Hit** | a hit | It works. An Appeal or an Argument gets "yes, but": their nature names the price; you pay, or you walk away. |
| **Miss** | a miss | They hit back their own way, and it hurts: you take their nature's **emotion** (of the ways to read someone, only a Provoke). Whether they'll hear the same thing again is the GM's call. |
| **Bad miss** | 5 under, or a 1 | As a miss, and they take a String on you. If they believe a lie of yours, it comes out. |

**In private, or before witnesses.** Before an ask or a gambit, say who is watching. Before witnesses a no shames you (you lose face: Insecure). For a whole court, the GM makes the scene public from the Social Scene's header, and every ask there starts before witnesses. And the room tips the roll: some natures bend before an audience (+2), most harden (−2). Like the key and the wall, that shows only in the result, or in a Read.

After a miss you may burn your String on them for +5 (that roll then moves no Strings). Your roll adds:
- the better of the maneuver's two skills;
- +1 per dot of your Leaning in that way, −1 on a way with no dots while you lean elsewhere (Profile);
- your proficiency bonus if you're trained in the other, so it grows with you;
- −2 for an emotion of yours that spoils your approach (−1 for its scar) — and **advantage** for an emotion of theirs that your approach plays on (against their wall the two cancel out: a normal roll);
- −2 (on the DC: +2) if your last Question against them missed — they're on their guard, for that one roll;
- +● for a bond that runs that way, or whose skill rolls (−● for a skill it forbids, and a hostile one's −● on anything but Pressure);
- +2 for their known Desire or Fear;
- +2 when another character really backed you up in this talk (they don't roll, and nothing lands on them).

## Emotions and scars

Five emotions, as in Thirsty Sword Lesbians. Anyone can carry them, a player character or an NPC, and each is a state of the whole person — toward everyone. They show as status badges on the token (and, in Level Up, in the system's effects panel); the GM can also toggle them from the Token HUD.

Each is **one flaw and one opening**:
- **on whoever feels it** — −2 on one way of talking, and −2 on one roll in a fight, on the sheet by itself;
- **for whoever plays on it** — that approach rolls with **advantage**. Against their wall the two cancel out, as advantage and disadvantage do in D&D: a normal roll. A Frightened Tyrant can be pressed; a Guilty Duelist can be asked from the heart. Say something that leaves the emotion first, then ask.

| Emotion | On whoever feels it | Whoever plays on it | Act it out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Angry** | Heart −2 · AC −2 | Pressure — advantage | lash out: hurt someone, or break something |
| **Frightened** | Pressure −2 · attack rolls −2 | Pressure — advantage | back down, or flee what scares you |
| **Guilty** | all-ins and lies −2 · initiative −2 | Heart — advantage | make amends |
| **Insecure** | Reason −2 · saving throws −2 | Heart — advantage | prove yourself with a reckless risk |
| **Hopeless** | plain asks −2 · damage −2 | Reason — advantage | give something up, or accept comfort |

**How it goes.** It weighs on you until you **act it out** in the scene — do what it pushes you to (the last column), then click it: it goes, and your character takes D&D **Inspiration**, the way the game rewards playing a flaw. A friend's comfort lets it go too, with no reward. Get the same emotion again while you still carry it, and it leaves a **scar**.

Where emotions come from:
- **the talk itself**: your words land, and the GM marks it from the NPC's own Talk tab — no roll (marking it again takes it back);
- **a miss**: each nature hits back with its own emotion (the Tyrant frightens you, the Duelist makes you angry, the Martyr makes you guilty…);
- **an NPC's move**, refused (below);
- **gambits**: Blackmail leaves them Frightened; a Seduce that leaned on your bond and failed breaks your heart (Hopeless); a lie brought out leaves them Angry;
- **someone close, used**: winning something against the interests of a friend, family or a lover leaves you Guilty, whatever the way;
- **a no before witnesses**: you lose face, and you're Insecure.

A **scar** is permanent, one per emotion: −1 on the same two things as its emotion. No roll takes it off — when the story has truly healed it, the GM (or you, on your own character) clicks it off.

| Scar | From | What it keeps, permanently |
|---|---|---|
| Short Fuse | Angry | Heart −1 · AC −1 |
| Flinch | Frightened | Pressure −1 · attack rolls −1 |
| Debt | Guilty | all-ins and lies −1 · initiative −1 |
| Two Faces | Insecure | Reason −1 · saving throws −1 |
| Hollow | Hopeless | plain asks −1 · damage −1 |

## Nine natures

The natures follow Karen Horney's three ways of coping with people:
- moving **against** them — Power: Tyrant, Schemer, Duelist;
- moving **toward** them — Emotion: Martyr, Idol, Caretaker;
- moving **away** from them — Order: Zealot, Hermit, Broker.

Each nature is grounded in a real character type. Each has:
- a **key** approach: on its maneuvers (the ask, the gambit, a lie told that way) you roll with **advantage** — and in the talk, the GM plays them opening up to it;
- a **wall** approach: **disadvantage**, and your second skill doesn't back you there (the third approach is a normal roll);
- its own **price** for a "yes, but";
- how it hits back when you miss, and the emotion that leaves on you;
- what such a person wants and dreads, when the GM wrote nothing of their own;
- how it takes an audience: before witnesses it bends (+2), hardens (−2), or doesn't care.

The second die is rolled after the first, so nothing before the roll gives the nature away; the result does. Inside a triad all three key/wall pairs differ, so knowing someone's way is never enough.

**Reading people.** Say how you read them — each way finds its own, and costs its own on a miss:

| You… | It finds | If you miss |
|---|---|---|
| **Watch** — say little, watch how they answer | is it true · what they're after · how they act before witnesses | nothing: watching is safe |
| **Provoke** — needle them until they say too much | their Fear · what closes them · is it true | they hit back: their emotion on you |
| **Confide** — open up, so they open up too | their Desire · what opens them · what they're after | you showed your heart: a String on you |
| **Question** — press their story until it stops adding up | their Price · how they act before witnesses · is it true | they're on their guard: your next roll against them is 2 harder |

A read by an approach meets their nature like anything said that way — their key gives advantage, their wall disadvantage, and the dice show which. No read wins a String.

A hit answers one question for the whole party (a strong hit, two):
- what they want;
- what they fear;
- what would open them;
- what would close them;
- what they'd want in return;
- whether they bend or harden before witnesses;
- and, whenever it matters, what they're after right now and whether what they just said is true (about the moment, so these two aren't kept).

**Something to go on, always.** Every answer comes with a line of what you see — one of twenty for each nature and each question, 1260 in all, so a Read gives the players something even when the GM wrote nothing about this person: for what they hide, the hint they name it from (“an unexpected kindness makes them look for the hook in it”); for what opens or closes them, how it looks in the scene; for what they're after, with no Agenda written, the answer itself. Where the GM wrote their own Desire, Fear or Agenda, the GM plays that instead, and the whisper says how.

Every Read that hits also notices one thing about them — a sign of who they really are, one of forty per nature (“You notice: they test small commands to see who jumps”). None of it names the nature: it's evidence for the table.

**Signs, and your guess.** The party keeps every sign it has noticed — Talk lists them under what it knows — and the GM can let more show in the talk, with no roll (the NPC's Talk tab offers three at a time out of forty). From the signs the party puts a guess forward: *Who are they, you think?* Pick a nature, and its hint shows, the approach buttons pencil in how they'd take each way if you're right, and the odds on the Roll button follow it, marked with a “?”. The dice follow the truth: the card says when a roll doesn't fit the guess. The GM sees the guess, right or wrong, and can confirm a right one.

The party keeps every answer. Once it knows the key and the wall, the nature is named and its whole card opens — with how that nature shows in plain sight.

## Strings and bonds

A **String** is what you have on someone: a debt, a weakness seen, a line crossed in front of others. **One at a time**: you have something on them, or you don't.

- **How it moves.** The roll the GM calls is its stake: a strong win (5 over, or a 20) and you take one on them; a bad miss (5 under, or a 1) and they take one on you. A plain win or a plain miss moves none. Any miss from the heart gives them one too (an Appeal, a Seduce). Some wins cost one back: Pressure and Blackmail (they'll pay you back), a Seduce (you let them close), a price that is a debt. A Read moves none, and neither does a roll on which a String was spent. The GM can also hand one over from the talk: a confession, a secret, a favour owed.
- **Spending yours:**
  - **they agree**, instead of the roll (Talk → *Remind them*): a small or costly ask is done; one against their interests, the GM says what it would take;
  - **+5** after a miss against them;
  - **invest** it in your bond (Bonds → *Invest*): +1 ●, or −1 ● on a hostile one (making peace); with no bond yet, you choose what you become to each other;
  - **Pull** it in a fight: Strike gives +5 to your next attack, check or save against them; Guard gives +5 AC against their next attack. Either is a real effect on your sheet that ends itself;
  - **Quits**: when they pull theirs on you, answer with yours, and both burn.
  Holding one is something to lean on for an ask against their interests, or for Blackmail.
- **Bonds** are one shared relationship per pair, kept on both sides: what a player marks on their character, the NPC shows too (the GM writes the other side). A bond has a **type**, which is who you are to each other (friend, lover, rival, enemy, sworn, confidant…), and a **strength** ●–●●●, which is how deep it runs. It also has a caption for the relationship map.
  - **The type is the story's.** The GM sets it, or you do when you invest a String in a new bond. A roll never changes it. An exposed lie makes strangers Enemies. Anything else, the GM changes when the story does.
  - **The dots are its depth.** They grow only when you invest a String (or by the GM's hand); a player can lower them freely.
  - **Their guard**: their bond toward you lowers the DC when you ask them (−1 per ● for someone close), or raises it for an Enemy (+1 per ●).
  - **Per ●, against that person**, each type gives its own: +1 on the approach it runs (Heart for love and family, Pressure for rivals and enemies, Reason for allies, oaths and trust), +1 when its skill rolls (a Friend: Persuasion; Family: Insight), −1 when the skill it forbids rolls (you can't frighten a lover, or fool your teacher). A Rival or an Enemy won't hear you out: −1 per ● on anything but Pressure. Press someone close to you (a friend, family, a lover…) and you win, but you're Guilty.
  - **Close by** — in a fight or not — standing close (15 ft by default, a world setting; 0 turns it off) gives the bond's aura on both sheets by itself, and it comes off when you step apart or leave the map. Every type gives something for a fight, and it grows with the bond: +1 a ● on its own thing, and a second one from ●● — a Lover +1/+2/+3 AC (and +1 to attack rolls from ●●), Family AC and then saves, an Ally attack rolls and then AC, a Rival attack rolls and then initiative, an Enemy damage and then initiative but −1 to saves, a Mentor attack rolls and then your spell DC… No type gives attack rolls and damage both, so a number on a status is the whole of it. It all goes into the rolls by itself: dnd5e adds it, and Level Up's roll window lists it ticked. Several bonds sum, capped at ±3 on one thing. Reach is counted between the tokens' edges, so a dragon next to you is next to you.
  - **You see it on the token.** While you stand within that reach, the bond is a status on both of you, next to the others: the type's badge, in the colour of the way the bond runs (Pressure, Heart or Reason), named with its dots and the other one, and the aura is in it. A token the GM hid doesn't count. It shows between any two, NPCs too. The status's own text says the rest: what the aura gives right now, their guard, the Strings both ways, and whether the ●● ability and the ●●● signature are ready. It is one of the system's own statuses, so the system lists it wherever it lists the others — in Level Up (a5e), its effects panel in the corner of the screen, for the token you control. The module opens no window of its own on the map. The first tab of the Chronicle lists the same statuses under *Close by*.
  - **At ●●** the type's own ability opens, **once per long rest** (Bonds → *Use it*), and it does what it says by itself: In sync (+2 on your next roll and theirs), Steady me (let an emotion go — or temporary hit points), Blood answers (temporary hit points for both of you), Wordless (your lover has advantage on their next roll), Forewarned (temporary hit points against your enemy)…
  - **At ●●●**, its signature, **once per long rest** too (Bonds → *Call on it*): I've got you (a friend's failed roll, rolled again), Blood shields blood, For the oath (advantage and +5)… Those that are numbers go on the sheet until your next roll; the rest the GM plays.
  - **A long rest readies both by itself** (in dnd5e and in Level Up); the GM can ready one early.

## What an NPC does with a String

Each NPC has three moves, and each costs the String they hold on that character.

- **At your roll — their triad's.** After the dice the GM is asked whether to spend it. **Power** strikes back: −5. **Emotion** makes you feel it: you win, and carry their emotion. **Order** sets the terms: you win, and pay their price.
- **In the talk, and beyond the scene — their nature's two.** The GM uses them from the NPC's Talk tab. A chat card asks the player for an answer (give in, and take Inspiration; or refuse, and pay for it), or the GM marks how a deadline ended. If you hold a String on them, you can answer with it: Quits.

| Nature | In the talk | Beyond the scene |
|---|---|---|
| Tyrant | An order before witnesses | An example made: kneel by next session, or a friend pays |
| Schemer | A hook: do the small favour (and they keep the String), or Insecure | I know all about you: a lie of yours comes out |
| Duelist | A challenge: decline (Insecure), or accept and roll for the String | Cut you off: your next goal, at disadvantage |
| Martyr | After everything I've done for you… | I can't do without you: come, or Guilt and a Rival |
| Idol | The room is mine | The crowd turns: Insecure in their circle |
| Caretaker | You'd be lost without me: any failed roll, rerolled — and you owe them again | Jealous care: a bond of yours cools |
| Zealot | Your sin: penance, or Angry | The law demands: by next session, or Enemies |
| Hermit | One question: answer truly, or Hopeless | Silence: every ask +4 DC until you bring a truth |
| Broker | An old debt: pay, or Hopeless (and the debt stands) | A debt for sale: their String goes to someone else |

A move is a tell: the players see its name, and an NPC moves as who they really are. Inspired by Thirsty Sword Lesbians' GM moves for each playbook.

## Windows

- **Chronicle**: right-click a token, then the address-book button. At the top of every tab: what weighs on them right now — their emotions and scars, by name. **×** lets one go; **Change** opens every emotion and scar as a tile — the GM on anyone, a player on their own character. No roll. The **?** beside the tabs shows the hints — what each part of the window is for; they're off until you ask. The system's own conditions (Prone, Charmed…) stay with the system: the token HUD. It has four tabs:
  - *Profile* — the first tab, where the Chronicle opens: what weighs on them now — every emotion (and, on a character, every scar) as a tile, lit while it's on them with what it costs; click a dim one to put it on, a lit one to let it go (the GM on anyone, a player on their own character) — and *Close by* — the bonds in reach, the same statuses as on the token; the Strings at hand (who on this map you have something on, with Remind them, Strike +5, Guard +5 AC and Invest right there); a player character's **Leanings — their archetype** (dots in Power, Emotion and Order — 4 to spread, at most 3 in one; each dot is +1 to two social skills on the sheet, on every check — Power: Intimidation and Deception, Emotion: Insight and Persuasion, Order: Deception and Persuasion; a skill two share adds both, up to +3 — and +1 on maneuvers of its way, −1 on a way with no dots while leaning elsewhere) and their maneuvers (what each one rolls for them, best first); an NPC's nature, dossier and Agenda for the GM (an empty Desire or Fear shows their nature's own, so the GM knows what a Read will show), and what the party has learned for players;
  - *Bonds*: every relationship at once — the type, the dots and the Strings both ways (ticked when they're there), with nothing to open. A String is a switch: click it to mark it or take it off by hand — a player on their own character (the NPC's side of it goes through the GM), the GM on anyone; the GM also changes the type and the dots right in the row. Everything the bond gives is in sight in its row: per ● against them, their guard, its aura, its ●● ability and its ●●● signature (*Call on it*), with Invest and Pull; only the caption for the map and your notes fold away. Add a bond with anyone — by name, by dragging an actor in from the sidebar, or from the map;
  - **Talk**: who you're talking to (your target on the map), and right under their name the Strings between you — ticked when you have one on them (*Remind them* spends yours instead of a roll); their emotions; and, when the GM calls for a roll, *I persuade*, *I intimidate*, *I lie* or *I read them* — how your words came at them, plainly or all in, in private or before witnesses — with your odds on the roll button. On an NPC's own sheet this tab is the GM's live screen: how they take each approach (what to act out), price, how they hit back, what they want and fear; their Agenda and their Line; what the talk revealed and the emotions it left, on them and on the characters here (marked here, no roll); the lies they believe; their three moves, with a button to pull a String on whoever they hold one on; and buttons to ask them as any character on the scene;
  - *Codex*: the in-game manual — how to play, the roll, people, emotions, Strings, bonds, and a page for the GM; each opens with one plain sentence, then steps and tables.
- **Social Scene**: the **Social** button on the VTools toolbar. A relationship map for everyone:
  - portraits with their names (and a caption under the name), bonds as lines (thicker = stronger, dashed = hostile) labelled with the bond and its caption, Strings as gold arrows from who holds one to whom, emotions and named natures;
  - drag a portrait or a caption to move it, drag the background to pan, the wheel zooms; a click shows who someone is and what ties them (the Chronicle opens from its button there);
  - captions pinned to the board — a place, a faction, a rumour — for everyone;
  - where the GM puts people, everyone sees; a player's drags arrange only their own view;
  - every roll is animated as it happens, and the GM can make the scene public here.
  *This scene* is for the table; *All bonds* is for the GM.
- **Bonds in reach**: on the tokens, as statuses of the system's own kind (the GM's client writes them, so a GM must be connected). Emotions are the same. In Level Up they show in the system's effects panel too.

## Settings

- **Language / Мова** (each user's own): Auto follows Foundry's language; or English, or Ukrainian. It switches at once. Chat cards are written in the language of whoever creates them (the GM's client resolves rolls).
- A global social-difficulty dial, and whether to roll in the system's own check window (on by default: it opens already set to the maneuver's skill and bonus; off rolls at once, the same way).
- **Bond aura reach**: how close (in feet) you stand to someone you have a bond with for its aura to count, in a fight or not. 15 by default; 0 turns bond auras off (the bond still shows on the map within 15 ft).

Updating to 3.3 adds two script files, and 3.9 two more (`abilities.js`, `bond-powers.js`): **restart the world** (not just F5) after updating.

Upgrading from 2.x: the first world load removes the old v2 states, wounds, scars and auras from sheets; v3's emotions and scars replace them. Bonds, Strings, dossiers and natures are kept.
