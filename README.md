# TSL: Social Conflict

Social conflict for **Foundry VTT v13+** (v14 verified), built for **dnd5e** and **a5e** (Level Up). You want something from an NPC who doesn't want to give it, and the person matters more than the dice. Every NPC has a hidden **psychological nature**: a **key** that opens them, a **wall** they close against, and a **price** they name when they give in. Read them first, and a modest character beats a silver tongue who never looked. Many wear a **mask**, and some bend or harden **before witnesses**. Inspired by the emotional mechanics of **Thirsty Sword Lesbians**, with a court-intrigue edge:

> *Every word is a move. Every favour is a debt. Everyone wears a mask — and someone is always watching.*

The module speaks **English and Ukrainian** (a per-user setting).

## A conversation is played

You want something from someone who doesn't want to give it. No turns, no tracks, no score — and the conversation itself isn't run by the module: **you play it**. The players talk in character with someone on the map (hover their token, press **T**); the GM answers as them.

1. **Talk.** The GM plays their hidden nature: one approach opens them, another closes them — the NPC's own Talk tab says how they take each one. Watch how they take what you say: that's how you read them.
2. **What the talk does, stays — no roll.** When something comes out (what they want, what they fear, their price), the GM marks it and the party knows it for good. When your words land — a threat that frightens, a reproach that shames, a truth with no way out — the GM marks the emotion it leaves on them: +2 to the approach that plays on it, until they act it out.
3. **The roll — when the GM calls for it.** Usually once, at the moment that decides it; at most two or three times a conversation. Talk asks what you're doing — *I persuade*, *I intimidate*, *I lie* or *I read them*, the four social checks of D&D — and what happened: how your words came at them, plainly or all in, what you asked for, who saw it, what you played on, who backed you — and counts all of it.

Each ask is its own yes or no. What carries over is what you learn about them, the emotions you leave, the Strings and bonds.

## Eight maneuvers

When the GM calls for the roll, say what your words really were, and roll once against a **visible DC = 10 + their proficiency + the better of WIS/INT**. **Every maneuver is a pair of D&D skills**: the better of the two rolls, in the system's own check window (a5e: expertise dice and effects count; change the ability or add dice there; on dnd5e the module rolls the d20 itself, with the same math); the other **backs it (+ your proficiency)** if you're trained in it. The module keeps no stats of its own — your sheet is your character, and your Profile lists what you're best at.

| Button | Maneuvers |
|---|---|
| **I persuade** | **Appeal** (Heart) — Persuasion + Performance · **Argument** (Reason) — Persuasion + History · all in: **Seduce** (Heart) — Persuasion + Performance · **Deal** (Reason) — Persuasion + Insight |
| **I intimidate** | **Demand** (Pressure) — Intimidation + Athletics · all in: **Blackmail** (Pressure) — Intimidation + Investigation |
| **I lie** | **Deceive**, told any way — Deception + Performance: a threat you can't back up, feelings you don't have, proof that isn't |
| **I read them** | **Read them**, when the talk won't tell — Insight + Investigation, *no approach* |

- **Asks** are sized by what they cost *them*:

  | Size | What it is | DC |
  |---|---|---|
  | Small | a favour, a door | −4 |
  | Costly | something it costs them to give | — |
  | Against them | against their interests or loyalties | +4 |

  An ask against them needs leverage: their Desire or Fear, a String on them, or a deep bond — or go all in.
- **All in** works at any size, on its own leverage, but it leaves a lasting mark:
  - **Blackmail** plays on their Fear: they do it, Frightened of you, and they'll pay you back.
  - **Seduce** plays on their Desire or your bond: they do it for you.
  - **A Deal** stakes your word (their Price, if you know it, adds +2): you owe them until you deliver.
- **A lie isn't an approach — it's told in one.** Press *I lie* and say how you told it, and how big it is (a small lie is 4 easier, a bold one 4 harder): their key and wall, your bond and their emotions all count for the way you told it. A sob story to someone who opens to Heart rolls with advantage; forged proof to someone who closes against Reason, with disadvantage. **Deceive** works until the GM brings the lie out — then they turn on you.
- **Read them** answers one question for the whole party; a strong hit, two. A Read never moves Strings, and a missed one never costs you an emotion.

**Every roll is a stake.** Win an ask, an all-in or a lie by 5 or more (or roll a 20), and you take a **String** on them; miss by 5 or more (or roll a 1), and they take one on you. The same rule both ways, one String at a time each way. A roll never changes a bond. What winning also costs depends on the approach:

| Approach | When it works | When it misses |
|---|---|---|
| **Pressure** | They give in without a price, but remember it: they take a String on you, even when you win. Pressing someone close to you leaves you Guilty. | Their emotion. |
| **Heart** | Your heart is on the table: a plain hit costs their price. | You showed your heart: their emotion. |
| **Reason** | They see it your way; a plain hit costs their price. A Deal stakes your word: they hold a String on you until you deliver. Keep it and their String goes; break it and yours on them burns. The GM marks which. | Their emotion. |

What they say:

| Result | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong hit** | 5 over, or a 20 | It works, on your terms: no price. You take a String on them. |
| **Hit** | a hit | It works. An Appeal, an Argument or a Deal gets "yes, but": their nature names the price; you pay, or you walk away. |
| **Miss** | a miss | They push back their own way, and it hurts: you take their nature's **emotion** (anything but a Read). A proud nature, refused something big, closes the door for the scene. |
| **Bad miss** | 5 under, or a 1 | As a miss, and they take a String on you. |

**In private, or before witnesses.** Before an ask or a gambit, say who is watching. Before witnesses a no shames you (you lose face: Insecure). For a whole court, the GM makes the scene public from the Social Scene's header, and every ask there starts before witnesses. And the room tips the roll: some natures bend before an audience (+2), most harden (−2). Like the key and the wall, that shows only in the result, or in a Read.

After a miss you may burn your String on them for +5 (that roll then moves no Strings). Your roll adds:
- the better of the maneuver's two skills;
- +1 per dot of your Leaning in that way, −1 on a way with no dots while you lean elsewhere (Profile);
- your proficiency bonus if you're trained in the other, so it grows with you;
- +2 for an emotion they feel that plays into your approach (−2 for one of yours that spoils it);
- +● for a bond that runs that way, or whose skill rolls (−● for a skill it forbids, and a hostile one's −● on anything but Pressure);
- +2 for their known Desire or Fear;
- +2 when another character really backed you up in this talk (they don't roll, and nothing lands on them).

## Emotions and scars

Five emotions, as in Thirsty Sword Lesbians. Anyone can carry them, a player character or an NPC. They show as status icons on the token; the GM can also toggle them from the Token HUD.

| Emotion | On whoever feels it | For whoever plays on it | Let it go |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Angry** | −2 on Heart | +2 to Pressure against them | lash out: hurt someone, or break something |
| **Frightened** | −2 on Pressure | +2 to Pressure against them | back down, or flee what scares you |
| **Guilty** | −2 on everything against the one you wronged | +2 to Heart from the one they wronged | make amends |
| **Insecure** | −2 on Reason | +2 to Heart against them | prove yourself with a reckless risk |
| **Hopeless** | −2 on asks and gambits | +2 to Reason against them | give something up, or accept comfort |

A friend's comfort lets any of them go, too. Where emotions come from:
- **the talk itself**: your words land, and the GM marks it from the NPC's own Talk tab — no roll (marking it again takes it back; Guilty is marked toward one character);
- **a miss**: each nature hits back with its own emotion (the Tyrant frightens you, the Duelist makes you angry, the Martyr makes you guilty…);
- **an NPC's move**, refused (below);
- **gambits**: Blackmail leaves them Frightened; a broken heart leaves you Hopeless; a lie brought out leaves them Angry;
- **a no before witnesses**: you lose face, and you're Insecure.

Take an emotion you already carry, and it leaves a **scar** instead. A scar is permanent, one per emotion, and a twist rather than just a wound:

| Scar | From | For good |
|---|---|---|
| Short Fuse | Angry | +1 Pressure, −1 Heart |
| Flinch | Frightened | +1 on Reads, −1 Pressure |
| Debt | Guilty | +1 Heart, −1 Reason |
| Two Faces | Insecure | +1 on Gambits, −1 on Reads |
| Hollow | Hopeless | +1 Reason, −1 Heart |

## Nine natures

The natures follow Karen Horney's three ways of coping with people:
- moving **against** them — Power: Tyrant, Schemer, Duelist;
- moving **toward** them — Emotion: Martyr, Idol, Caretaker;
- moving **away** from them — Order: Zealot, Hermit, Broker.

Each nature is grounded in a real character type. Each has:
- a **key** approach: on its maneuvers (the ask, the gambit, a lie told that way) you roll with **advantage** — and in the talk, the GM plays them opening up to it;
- a **wall** approach: **disadvantage**, and your second skill doesn't back you there (the third approach is a normal roll);
- its own **price** for a "yes, but";
- its own pushback on a no, and the emotion it leaves on you when you miss;
- whether it keeps listening after refusing something big;
- how it takes an audience: before witnesses it bends (+2), hardens (−2), or doesn't care.

The second die is rolled after the first, so nothing before the roll gives the nature away; the result does. Inside a triad all three key/wall pairs differ, so knowing someone's way is never enough.

**Reading people.** A hit answers one question for the whole party:
- what they want;
- what they fear;
- what would open them;
- what would close them;
- what they'd want in return;
- whether they end it after a no;
- whether the face they show is their true one;
- whether they bend or harden before witnesses;
- and, whenever it matters, what they're after right now and whether what they just said is true (about the moment, so these two aren't kept).

The party keeps every answer. Once it knows the key and the wall, the nature is named and its whole card opens.

**Masks** (Nature and Demeanor). The GM can give an NPC a face to show: their own nature (an open face), another one (a mask: a Schemer playing the Caretaker), or nothing (unreadable). Players see "seems: Caretaker" in Talk and on the Scene, and can open how they seem; the key, the wall and the price are always the true nature's. A Read ("Is that their true face?") names an open face on the spot, or shows a mask for what it is; learning the key and the wall names them, and the mask falls.

## Strings and bonds

A **String** is what you have on someone: a debt, a weakness seen, a line crossed in front of others. **One at a time**: you have something on them, or you don't.

- **How it moves.** The roll the GM calls is its stake: a strong win (5 over, or a 20) and you take one on them; a bad miss (5 under, or a 1) and they take one on you. A plain win or a plain miss moves none. Some wins cost one back: Pressure and Blackmail (they'll pay you back), a Deal (your word, until you deliver), a price that is a debt. A Read moves none, and neither does a roll on which a String was spent. The GM can also hand one over from the talk: a confession, a secret, a favour owed.
- **Spending yours:**
  - **they agree**, instead of the roll (Talk → *Remind them*): a small or costly ask is done; one against their interests, the GM says what it would take;
  - **+5** after a miss against them;
  - **invest** it in your bond (Bonds → *Invest*): +1 ●, or −1 ● on a hostile one (making peace); with no bond yet, you choose what you become to each other;
  - **Pull** it in a fight: Strike gives +5 to your next attack, check or save against them; Guard gives +5 AC against their next attack. Either is a real effect on your sheet that ends itself;
  - **Quits**: when they pull theirs on you, answer with yours, and both burn.
  Holding one is leverage for an ask against their interests, or for Blackmail.
- **Bonds** are one shared relationship per pair, kept on both sides: what a player marks on their character, the NPC shows too (the GM writes the other side). A bond has a **type**, which is who you are to each other (friend, lover, rival, enemy, sworn, confidant…), and a **strength** ●–●●●, which is how deep it runs. It also has a caption for the relationship map.
  - **The type is the story's.** The GM sets it, or you do when you invest a String in a new bond. A roll never changes it. A word kept in a Deal makes strangers Allies; an exposed lie or a broken word makes them Enemies. Anything else, the GM changes when the story does.
  - **The dots are its depth.** They grow only when you invest a String (or by the GM's hand); a player can lower them freely.
  - **Their guard**: their bond toward you lowers the DC when you ask them (−1 per ● for someone close), or raises it for an Enemy (+1 per ●).
  - **Per ●, against that person**, each type gives its own: +1 on the approach it runs (Heart for love and family, Pressure for rivals and enemies, Reason for allies, oaths and trust), +1 when its skill rolls (a Friend: Persuasion; Family: Insight), −1 when the skill it forbids rolls (you can't frighten a lover, or fool your teacher). A Rival or an Enemy won't hear you out: −1 per ● on anything but Pressure. Press someone close to you (a friend, family, a lover…) and you win, but Guilty toward them.
  - **Close by** — in a fight or not — standing close (15 ft by default, a world setting; 0 turns it off) gives the bond's aura on your sheet by itself, and it comes off when you step apart or leave the map: Family closes ranks (+1 attack and saves), a Lover +2 AC and +1 initiative, a Mentor +2 to your spell DC, an Enemy +2 attack and initiative but −1 saves… Each doubles at ●●●, never past ±2; several bonds sum, capped at ±2.
  - **You see it on the token.** While you stand within that reach, the bond is a status on both of you, next to the others: the type's icon, tinted the way the bond runs (Pressure, Heart or Reason), named with its dots and the other one, and the aura is in it. A token the GM hid doesn't count. Between two NPCs it works on the sheet but stays off the token, so an NPC's secret ally doesn't show. The first tab of the Chronicle lists the same statuses under *Close by*. A panel in the bottom-left corner, above the players, lists everyone in reach with what the bond gives right now: the aura, their guard, the Strings both ways, the ●● ability, and whether the ●●● signature is ready. Click a name there to open your bonds; click the header to fold it.
  - **At ●●** the type's own ability opens (Wordless: you always know when your lover lies; Forewarned: your enemy can't surprise you…).
  - **At ●●●**, its signature, **once per long rest** (Bonds → *Call on it*): I've got you (a friend's failed roll, rolled again), Blood shields blood, For the oath (advantage and +5)… Those that are numbers go on the sheet until your next roll; the rest the GM plays. The GM can ready it early.

## What an NPC does with a String

Each NPC has three moves, and each costs the String they hold on that character.

- **At your roll — their triad's.** After the dice the GM is asked whether to spend it. **Power** strikes back: −5. **Emotion** makes you feel it: you win, and carry their emotion. **Order** sets the terms: you win, and pay their price.
- **In the talk, and beyond the scene — their nature's two.** The GM uses them from the NPC's Talk tab. A chat card asks the player for an answer (give in, and take Inspiration; or refuse, and pay for it), or the GM marks how a deadline ended. If you hold a String on them, you can answer with it: Quits.

| Nature | In the talk | Beyond the scene |
|---|---|---|
| Tyrant | An order before witnesses | An example made: kneel by next session, or a friend pays |
| Schemer | A hook: do the small favour (and they keep the String), or Insecure | I know all about you: a lie of yours comes out |
| Duelist | A challenge: decline (Insecure), or accept and roll for the String | Cut you off: your next goal, at disadvantage |
| Martyr | After everything I've done for you… | I can't do without you: come, or Guilt and a Rival |
| Idol | The room is mine | The crowd turns: Insecure in their circle |
| Caretaker | You'd be lost without me: any failed roll, rerolled — and you owe them again | Jealous care: a bond of yours cools |
| Zealot | Your sin: penance, or Angry | The law demands: by next session, or Enemies |
| Hermit | One question: answer truly, or Hopeless | Silence: every ask +4 DC until you bring a truth |
| Broker | An old debt: pay, or Hopeless (and the debt stands) | A debt for sale: their String goes to someone else |

A move is a tell: the players see its name, and under a mask an NPC still moves as who they really are. Inspired by Thirsty Sword Lesbians' GM moves for each playbook.

## Windows

- **Chronicle**: right-click a token, then the address-book button. At the top of every tab: what weighs on them right now — their emotions and scars. **×** takes one off; **Change** opens every emotion and scar as a switch — the GM on anyone, a player on their own character. No roll. The system's own conditions (Prone, Charmed…) stay with the system: the token HUD. It has four tabs:
  - *Profile* — the first tab, where the Chronicle opens: what weighs on them now, with every emotion and scar as a switch right there (the GM on anyone, a player on their own character), and *Close by* — the bonds in reach, the same statuses as on the token; the Strings at hand (who on this map you have something on, with Remind them, Strike +5, Guard +5 AC and Invest right there); a player character's **Leanings — their archetype** (dots in Power, Emotion and Order — 4 to spread, at most 3 in one; each dot is +1 to a skill on the sheet — Intimidation, Insight, Deception, on every check — and +1 on maneuvers of its way, −1 on a way with no dots while leaning elsewhere) and their maneuvers (what each one rolls for them, best first); an NPC's nature, the mask they show, dossier and Agenda for the GM, and what the party has learned for players;
  - *Bonds*: every relationship at once — the type, the dots and the Strings both ways (ticked when they're there), with nothing to open. A String is a switch: click it to mark it or take it off by hand — a player on their own character (the NPC's side of it goes through the GM), the GM on anyone; the GM also changes the type and the dots right in the row. Everything the bond gives is in sight in its row: per ● against them, their guard, its aura, its ●● ability and its ●●● signature (*Call on it*), with Invest and Pull; only the caption for the map and your notes fold away. Add a bond with anyone — by name, by dragging an actor in from the sidebar, or from the map;
  - **Talk**: who you're talking to (your target on the map) and the face they show, and right under their name the Strings between you — ticked when you have one on them (*Remind them* spends yours instead of a roll); their emotions; and, when the GM calls for a roll, *I persuade*, *I intimidate*, *I lie* or *I read them* — how your words came at them, plainly or all in, in private or before witnesses — with your odds on the roll button. On an NPC's own sheet this tab is the GM's live screen: how they take each approach (what to act out), price, pushback, the mask to play; their Agenda and their Line; what the talk revealed and the emotions it left, on them and on the characters here (marked here, no roll); the word given to them (Kept or Broken); the lies they believe; their three moves, with a button to pull a String on whoever they hold one on; and buttons to ask them as any character on the scene;
  - *Codex*: the in-game manual — how to play, the roll, people, emotions, Strings, bonds, and a page for the GM; each opens with one plain sentence, then steps and tables.
- **Social Scene**: the **Social** button on the VTools toolbar. A relationship map for everyone:
  - portraits with their names (and a caption under the name), bonds as lines (thicker = stronger, dashed = hostile) labelled with the bond and its caption, Strings as gold arrows from who holds one to whom, emotions, named natures and the faces NPCs show;
  - drag a portrait or a caption to move it, drag the background to pan, the wheel zooms; a click shows who someone is and what ties them (the Chronicle opens from its button there);
  - captions pinned to the board — a place, a faction, a rumour — for everyone;
  - where the GM puts people, everyone sees; a player's drags arrange only their own view;
  - every roll is animated as it happens, and the GM can make the scene public here.
  *This scene* is for the table; *All bonds* is for the GM.
- **Bonds in reach**: on the tokens, as statuses (the GM's client writes them, so a GM must be connected), and in a panel in the bottom-left corner. A player's panel shows their own characters; the GM's shows the tokens they select.

## Settings

- **Language / Мова** (each user's own): Auto follows Foundry's language; or English, or Ukrainian. It switches at once. Chat cards are written in the language of whoever creates them (the GM's client resolves rolls).
- A global social-difficulty dial, and whether to roll in the system's own check window (on by default: it opens already set to the maneuver's skill and bonus; off rolls at once, the same way).
- **Bond aura reach**: how close (in feet) you stand to someone you have a bond with for its aura to count, in a fight or not. 15 by default; 0 turns bond auras off (the bond still shows on the map within 15 ft).
- **Bonds in reach: the corner panel** (each user's own; on by default). The bond itself shows on the tokens either way.

Updating to 3.3 adds two script files, and 3.9 two more (`abilities.js`, `bond-powers.js`): **restart the world** (not just F5) after updating.

Upgrading from 2.x: the first world load removes the old v2 states, wounds, scars and auras from sheets; v3's emotions and scars replace them. Bonds, Strings, dossiers and natures are kept.
